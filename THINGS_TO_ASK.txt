THINGS TO ASK MEYER TURKU
=========================
These are the open points behind the rules. The app no longer shows "assumed values" badges;
this file is where they live instead. The same questions are kept as data in rules.json
(questions_for_meyer), so update both when an answer comes in.

Q1  Fire integrity of walls (rule FIRE-WALL-RATING)
    Which fire integrity table do you use for room pairs (SOLAS II-2/9 tables 9.1 to 9.4)?
    Do you want us to follow it exactly? Please share it.
    Now: placeholder minutes per room pair in rules.json (engine room 60, galley 30, stairwell/cabin 30 ...).

Q2  Distance between two escape routes (rule ESCAPE-TWO-EXITS)
    How far apart must two escape routes be? Is there a number or a design rule you use?
    Now: 5 m between the first doors of the two routes. Regulation 13 only says "widely separated".

Q3  Escape stair width (rule ESCAPE-STAIR-WIDTH) - mostly answered by the USCG PDF
    The USCG guideline quotes FSS Code 13.2.1.1: escape stairs at least 900 mm. Regulation 13's 800 mm
    only applies to a dispensation case. Now: below 900 mm is red.
    To confirm: do you size stair width by the number of persons (FSS Code 13.2.1.2 widths per person)?
    We do not model that yet.

Q4  Evacuation analysis (rule EVACUATION-TIME)
    Values now come from MSC.1/Circ.1533 Annex 2 (downloaded). To confirm:
    - Is the simplified Annex 2 method acceptable at this stage, or do you run an advanced (agent) simulation?
    - E+L (embarkation and launching) is set to the 30 min default of Annex 1 para 5.5. Do you have a real value?
    - We assume 45 degree stairs and the narrowest corridor on a route as its clear width.

Q5  Flooding check (rule FLOOD-SURVIVAL)
    Which flooding survival limits do you want in a simplified check (heel angle, damage cases)?
    Is it fine to label our result a rough estimate?
    Now: single and adjacent-pair damage of full-breadth compartments, survive if GM > 0 and the
    bulkhead deck stays dry after sinkage and trim. Case weight = compartment length share.
    Permeabilities 0.95 accommodation, 0.85 machinery, 0.60 stores (SOLAS II-1/7-3 table, not in docs/).

Q6  Intact stability (rule INTACT-STABILITY)
    Should the check follow the IS Code 2008 general criteria only, or also the weather criterion and
    your internal margins?
    Now: the IS Code 2008 Part A 2.2 values as commonly quoted (the code itself is not in docs/, please confirm):
    area 0-30 >= 0.055 m.rad, 0-40 >= 0.09, 30-40 >= 0.03, GZ >= 0.20 m at 30 deg or more,
    max GZ at >= 25 deg, GM >= 0.15 m.

Q7  Survival craft (rule LSA-CAPACITY)
    Which arrangement do you design to: SOLAS III/21.1.1 (lifeboats for 50 % per side plus liferafts
    for 25 %) or a short international voyage / marine evacuation system arrangement?
    Now: 50 % per side + 25 % liferafts, craft must be on a deck with an assembly station.

Q8  Fire zone size (rule FIRE-ZONE-SIZE)
    We use 40 m for mean zone length and width (from the EU directive text). SOLAS allows up to 48 m
    in some cases. Which value do you work with?

Q9  Weights
    Can you share a typical lightship weight and KG for a ship like the demo, or a weight group
    breakdown, so the early estimate can be calibrated? The vessel.js Parsons estimate is rough.

Q10 Weight margins (rule WEIGHT-MARGIN)
    Which weight margins do you keep in early design?
    Now: design and build margin 6.8 % of lightship, service life margin 4.5 % (NOAA SWATH report 1990,
    Tables 1 and 8, NAVSEA practice). After adding them the freeboard must stay above 0.5 m (our value) and
    GM above the intact stability minimum.

Q11 Reference sizes (rule REFERENCE-SHIPS, amber hints only)
    What are your smallest cabin area per person and deck-to-deck height for ferries and cruise ships?
    Now: 7.5 m2 per person (2-person cabins of 15 m2 on GA 23007) and 2.5 m between decks (G.O. Sars).
    These come from research vessels, so passenger cabins may legitimately be smaller.

Q12 Model ships (src/data/ships.ts: Icon / Star / Legend of the Seas, Mein Schiff 7)
    Main data are published figures: Icon class 364.75 m overall, 48.47 m moulded breadth, 9.25 m draught,
    20 decks, 7,600 guests maximum, 2,350 crew; Mein Schiff 7 315.7 m, 35.8 m, 8.2 m draught (one source only),
    16 decks, 2,894 passengers, 1,000 crew. Everything else is our assumption, please correct:
    - Interior: simplified block layout (stair core at each end of every fire zone, central corridor, one room
      block per side). The real general arrangements are not public. Star and Legend differ from Icon only in
      galley positions and upper deck extent; we found no public data that tells them apart structurally.
    - Deck height 3.0 m, Deck 1 at 3.0 m above keel, bulkhead deck Deck 5, hull depth 21 m (Icon) / 15 m (MS7).
    - Lifeboat capacity 370 persons (Icon class, as on the Oasis class) and 150 (Mein Schiff 7), liferafts 150.
    - Lightship weight, LCG and KG are calibrated (scripts/calibrate-ships.ts) so the ship floats level at the
      published draught with GM 10.5 m. The vessel.js cruise hull form has a low block coefficient (about 0.41),
      so displacement is lower and GM higher than on the real ships; real lightship and KG would replace this.
    - The Icon class shows a red fire zone width (48.4 m against the 40 m in rules.json, see Q8; SOLAS
      II-2/9.2.2.1.1 allows up to 48 m in some cases). Kept on purpose: rules unchanged.
    - Stair queues (amber) come from the simplified Circ.1533 method with one assembly deck under ten guest
      decks; total evacuation time is well inside the limit.

Q13 Fire main (rule PIPE-FIRE-MAIN, docs/REG10_Fire_Fighting.pdf)
    What delivery pressure do your fire pumps have, and do you check hydrant pressure with friction losses?
    Now: 0.40 N/mm2 at every hydrant (Regulation 10, 2.1.6.1, passenger ships of 4,000 GT and upwards, from the
    document); 1.2 N/mm2 at the pumps (our value); static head only (rho g h from the engine room floor to the
    highest fire main), no friction. Every deck and fire zone with rooms must have the fire main on that deck.

Q14 Collision bulkhead (rule PIPE-WATERTIGHT)
    Which pipes do you route through the collision bulkhead below the bulkhead deck? Now: exactly one pipe for
    the forepeak (we take the bilge and ballast main), no cables. Regulation II-1/12 itself is not downloaded;
    the text comes from a Gard summary. Collision bulkhead = forwardmost watertight bulkhead forward of 0.7 L.

Q15 Fuel oil lines (rule PIPE-FUEL-HOT, docs/MSC_Circ647_Flammable_Leakage.pdf)
    What clear distance do you keep between fuel oil lines and exhaust pipes? Now: 1.0 m (our value; the
    circular gives no number, only "screened" and "clear of hot surfaces"). Fuel lines through cabins,
    corridors, public rooms, galleys and stairs are flagged amber: inferred good practice, not a quoted rule.

Q16 Cable routing (rule CABLE-ROUTING, docs/IACS_SC11_Cable_Routing_II-1_45.pdf)
    Which distance do you keep between main cables and machinery casings, and which spaces count as high fire
    risk? Now: 0.5 m from an exhaust uptake (our value); galleys, laundries (by name) and engine rooms other
    than the one the cable starts or ends in.

Q17 Default pipes and cables (src/engine/generators.ts defaultRuns)
    The default arrangement is a rough sketch from general knowledge, not a piping drawing: fire main along the
    corridor of every deck with rooms plus a riser from the engine room; bilge and ballast main on the tank top
    from the aft peak to the forepeak; fuel oil lines from the two fuel tanks nearest the engine room;
    exhaust uptakes from the two largest engine rooms straight up to the top deck; HV cable from the aftmost
    engine room to the propulsion motors aft; feeder riser plus one feeder per deck along the corridor.
    Sizes: fire main 200 mm, bilge 300 mm, fuel 100 mm, exhaust 900 mm, HV bundle 120 mm, feeder 80 mm.
    Pipes and cables add no weight to the stability calculation yet.
